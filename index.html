ABOUT

I am a first-year Health Decision Sciences PhD student at the University of Minnesota - Twin Cities Division of Health Policy & Management, advised by Eva Enns.

My research interests are primarily in preventing infectious diseases and improving vaccinations in low-middle-income countries. Some of my secondary research interests revolve around understanding human altruism in decision making and analyses aimed at understanding/creating better educational policies.

BACKGROUND

Prior to starting my PhD at UMN, I worked at the Midwest Analytics and Disease Modeling Center (MADMC) as a researcher. I developed models, dashboards around various pathogens like H5N1, RSV, Parvovirus B19, congenital syphilis, and measles. Specifically, I used models to help inform governmental agencies about the benefits and drawbacks of different interventions and strategies.

In my master's I worked with Dr. Ben Lopman to calculate reproductive numbers for 8 enteropathogens across 8 low-middle-income countries. I also worked with Dr. Sara Mirza at the CDC abstracting norovirus prevalence data.

During my bachelor's I worked with Dr. Caitilyn Allen where I studied drought tolerance on bacterial wilt resistant transgenic tomato plants.
